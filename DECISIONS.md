# Decisions and assumptions

Every assumption made while building the prototype. **Survey?** marks how likely the
HRMO survey is to change it:

- **Likely**: expected to change; built so it's easy to replace.
- **Maybe**: reasonable default, confirm with HRMO.
- **No**: technical or policy choice, unlikely to change.

---

## Survey findings (n=2, Oct 2026)

Responses from Municipal HRMO Head and Administrative Aide, plus follow-up interviews:

1. **Workforce Composition & Headcount:**
   - Total active personnel: ~414 to ~420.
   - Breakdown: ~94 permanent (Plantilla), ~28 casual, 12 elected (Mayor, Vice Mayor, 8 SB Members, ABC President, SK Federation President), ~280+ Job Order (JO) / Contract of Service (COS).
   - **JO/COS represents roughly two-thirds (67%) of the municipal workforce.**
2. **Current Operational Pain Points:**
   - Records are split across disconnected Excel and Google Sheets files: JO database, permanent database, service records, and leave credits.
   - Issues reported: duplicate employee profiles, conflicting entries across files, inaccurate leave balance computation, misplaced paper documents, and delayed reports.
3. **Legal & Document Reality:**
   - **Wet signatures and physical paper originals remain legally required.**
   - The system tracks physical archive locations (cabinet, drawer, folder); digital document uploads are digital scans/attachments, not paper replacements.
4. **Leave Approval Order (UNCONFIRMED):**
   - The Municipal Mayor (or authorized representative) signs **all** leave applications.
   - The exact step order between the two survey respondents differed (Supervisor recommendation vs HRMO credit certification vs Mayor approval). Built as a configurable approval chain.
5. **Leave Computation (UNVERIFIED):**
   - Leave credit computation formulas reported discrepancies.
   - No automatic monthly 1.25-day accrual. Credits are entered as baseline ledgers, and deduction occurs only upon final approved leave.
   - JO/COS generally do not earn paid leave; controlled explicitly via `Engagement.leave_eligible`.
6. **Attendance / DTR (Out of Scope):**
   - DTR is not strictly recorded in HRMO; pay is salary-based. No attendance module in Phase 1.
7. **Pass Slip (Deferred):**
   - Pass slip workflow: Employee → HRMO → Mayor's Office (or representative). Currently on a separate Excel sheet; deferred, but approval engine designed generically to support it.
8. **Parking Lot (To clarify later):**
   - Specific "Other" tasks mentioned by HRMO Head; Google Sheets leave and attendance coordination.

## Roles, permissions, and security

| # | Decision | Survey? |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Explicit Django permissions used for gating: `view_pay`, `view_ids`, `view_documents`, `approve_leave`, `view_hr_records`, `manage_hr_records`, `manage_projects` (P1). | No |
| R2 | **System Admin (IT) isolation:** Manages accounts and system settings in `/admin/`. Non-superusers cannot see or edit any HR models in `/admin/`. System admins have no access to HR records screens. | No |
| R3 | **Privilege Escalation Prevention:** Only the emergency superuser can assign the roles that grant HR record access (`HRMO Staff`, `Auditor`, `Executive`, `Office Head`, `Office Staff`, `Project Maintainer`, `Delegate`; `PROTECTED_ROLES`) or edit, delete and reset the passwords of any account that reaches HR records: their members, and also accounts with no role that still get in, which are employee self-service accounts (linked to a person), current or future delegates (R7) and accounts given an HR permission directly (`hr_access_accounts`). System Admin does not even see these accounts in `/admin/`; otherwise resetting an employee's password and logging in would show System Admin that person's PDS, IDs and leave. System Admin cannot self-escalate into HR access roles. A password an admin sets for another account (new account or reset) is one-time: `must_change_password` is set, so the user must change it at next login and an admin cannot quietly keep using the account. All account creations, password resets, and group changes are audit-logged. Exception: the `setup_roles` command, which creates the groups and their permission sets at deployment, runs with auditing off (owner-run provisioning, not a user action; *proven by* `SetupRolesAuditTestCase`). *Proven by* `SysadminEscalationTestCase` and `Step4CSecurityAndRolesTestCase.test_sysadmin_cannot_assign_protected_roles`. | No |
| R4 | **Data Minimization** (person, personnel list and office pages):<br>- Government IDs, birthdate, sex, home address: HRMO, Auditor, and self.<br>- Mobile and email: those plus Office Head and Office Staff (own office only).<br>- Document list (type, status, original on file, archive location): HRMO, Auditor, self, and Office Head. File names, notes and downloads: HRMO, Auditor, and self (including documents attached to the employee's own engagements).<br>- Engagement notes: HRMO and Auditor.<br>- Leave balances: HRMO, Auditor, self, and leave approvers (Office Head, Executive, Delegate).<br>- Office Staff: names, positions, contacts. No personal details, documents or pay.<br>- Executives & Delegates: name, position, office, engagement type (and, from Step 8, routed leave requests). No contacts, personal details, documents, IDs or pay.<br>*Enforced by the helpers in `hr/permissions.py`; proven by* `DataMinimizationTestCase`. | No |
| R5 | "Their office" = people with an **active** engagement in that office. Transferred staff are not visible to their past office heads. Roles stack (e.g. HRMO Head = Office Head + HRMO Staff). Past (ended) appointments on an office page are shown only to all-office roles (HRMO, Auditor, Executive, Delegate), so former staff do not appear to that office's head or staff (*proven by* `DataMinimizationTestCase.test_office_page_hides_salary_grade_and_past_staff_from_office_roles`). | Maybe |
| R6 | **Pay visibility (`view_pay`):** Pay rate, rate basis, salary grade and step are restricted to **HRMO Staff** and **Auditor** (salary grade + step gives the exact salary from the public SSL table). Hidden from Office Heads, Office Staff, Executives, Delegates, and Employees, including on their own record; the plantilla item number stays visible. *Proven by* `DataMinimizationTestCase` and `Step4CSecurityAndRolesTestCase`. | No |
| R7 | **Executive Delegate:** Time-bound delegation via `Delegate` model (`principal`, `delegate`, `valid_from`, `valid_to`). Access ends automatically when delegation expires. | No |
| R8 | Emergency Superuser: Emergency maintenance only. Routine administration uses role-based accounts. | No |
| R9 | **Every rule is enforced on the server.** Hiding a field, button or menu item in a page is presentation only; a user can add it back with the browser's developer tools or replay any request. So every view re-checks the role before acting, every query is scoped to what the role may see, forms drop fields the role may not set (rate and IDs) before reading the POST, fields that are not on a form (`employee_no`, `person`, `is_superuser`) are ignored if posted, choice fields accept only values from the server's own list (e.g. only funded open projects), and every POST needs a CSRF token. The only hidden field the app relies on is `version`, and tampering with it can only make a save fail. *Proven by* `TamperingTestCase` (extra fields, removed fields, other people's IDs, unreachable pages, the admin `is_superuser` box, missing CSRF token, off-site login redirect). | No |
| R10 | **HRMO never changes their own record (separation of duties).** An HRMO staff member who is also on the payroll cannot, on their own record: edit personal details, add, edit, end or renew appointments (salary grade, step, rate), add or change documents, add leave ledger entries, cancel their own approved leave (it would restore the days), credit their own overtime (COC), record their own trainings, enter their own SPMS rating, mark their own SALN received, work on their own salary action or service interruptions, or verify their own PDS. Another HRMO staff member does these; the screens leave the person out of pickers and show their row read-only. Everything else stays as self-service (filing leave, filling in the PDS). `can_manage_record_of` in `hr/permissions.py`. *Proven by* `HrmoOwnRecordTestCase`. | **Likely** (an HRMO with a single staff member needs the emergency superuser, or a second HRMO account, for their own record) |

## Leave

| # | Decision | Survey? |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | **Approval chain (built, Step 8):** each application gets one `LeaveApprovalStep` per signature box of section 7 of **CS Form No. 6 (Revised 2020)**, in the order set by `LEAVE_APPROVAL_STEPS` (default `recommend,certify,approve`): 1) the **Office Head** of the office the leave is filed in recommends (7.B), 2) **HRMO** certifies the credits (7.A) and the days with and without pay (7.C), 3) the **Mayor** or an active delegate approves (7.C/7.D). Only the step whose turn it is can be signed, by its role; nobody signs their own leave; disapproval at any step needs a reason and ends the application. If the applicant heads the office, the recommendation is skipped. The office and position are copied onto the application when it is filed, so a transfer does not move it. Each action uses the S2 lock and version check and is audit-logged. Order still **UNCONFIRMED** (survey finding 4): change the setting, not the code, once HRMO confirms. *Proven by* `LeaveWorkflowTestCase`. | **Likely** |
| L2 | **Credit deduction:** Simple ledger (`LeaveCredit`). Nothing is deducted until final approval; then the days with pay are charged: vacation and forced leave to VL; sick leave to SL first, the rest to VL (CSC rule); other leave types are not charged to a ledger. HRMO cancelling approved leave adds the days back as new rows (the ledger is never edited). Still no automatic accrual: HRMO presses **Post monthly credits** (1.25 VL + 1.25 SL to every active leave-eligible person, once per person per month, `Earned <Month YYYY>`), and adds beginning balances and corrections on the person's **leave card**. Computation formulas remain **UNVERIFIED**: check the leave card against the paper card before go-live. | **Likely** |
| L3 | **Leave eligibility:** Controlled by `Engagement.leave_eligible`, editable by HRMO for every type (including Plantilla and Casual set to False). The type default (True for Plantilla and Casual, False for others, `Engagement.default_leave_eligible()`) is only the engagement form's initial value, and the future importer must apply it explicitly; `Engagement.save()` never changes the stored value. *Proven by* `LeaveEligibleDefaultTestCase`. | No |
| L4 | **Leave types and days:** the 14 types of section 6.A of CS Form No. 6 (Revised 2020), including wellness leave (CSC MC No. 1, s. 2026, as printed on the 2026 form), plus Others (specify). Section 6.B details are offered per type (within the Philippines / abroad, in hospital / out patient, study purpose, monetization, terminal leave), with "specify" required where the form has a blank. Working days are suggested from the dates: weekdays less municipality-wide holidays on the calendar; the employee may change them for half days (whole or half days, up to the calendar span). Vacation, forced, sick and special privilege leave need an active leave-eligible appointment; the special leaves (maternity, paternity, solo parent, VAWC and others) do not, and HRMO checks entitlement. | **Likely** (confirm the wellness leave rules and whether JO/COS may file special leaves) |
| L5 | **What HRMO certifies:** the VL and SL balances on the day of certification (stored on the application for the printed 7.A) and the days with and without pay, worked out from what is available after other pending applications; special privilege leave is limited to 3 days a year. HRMO may change the split; it must add up to the days applied for. Days without pay are not yet copied to `ServiceInterruption` (SA2): HRMO records them on the salary case as today. | **Likely** |
| L6 | **Paper stays legal (F1):** every application prints as a filled-in CS Form No. 6 for wet signatures, with the names of whoever signed online. HRMO can enter a paper form for any employee in active service, and can record a recommendation or approval **from the signed paper** for a signatory without an account; such steps are marked "recorded by HRMO from paper". Printing is logged as an `EXPORT` (U1). The printout follows the LGU's 2026 paper form: the legal basis under each 6.A type, the officers' names under the signature lines (`LEAVE_FORM_CERTIFIER`, `LEAVE_FORM_CERTIFIER_TITLE`, `LEAVE_FORM_MAYOR`; blank by default, as the prototype stores no real names), a note of who signed online and when, and the instructions on page 2. Salary is left for the employee to write in (R6). Employees can **File and print** in one step, print any application from My leave, or print a **blank form** with their name, office and position to fill in by hand. | No |
| L7 | **Filing rules are warnings, not blocks:** vacation leave less than 5 days ahead, special privilege leave less than a week ahead, sick leave over 5 days or filed ahead (medical certificate), not enough credits, and leave already filed on the same days are shown to the employee and the signatories. The approvers decide. Each type shows the guidance from the form's instructions while filing. | **Likely** |
| L8 | **Online first:** the home page (**My HR**) shows each employee their available VL and SL (after days on hold), special privilege leave left this year, **forced leave still to take by December 31** (5 days when VL is 10 or more, counting VL and forced leave approved this year), their applications as a progress tracker, memos to acknowledge and open vacancies. Signatories get an inbox with a count on the menu and see who else from the office is away on the same days. HRMO and Auditor get the leave register (filters, Excel) and the **Leave balances as of** list in the bulletin-board layout (print, Excel), always current. *Proven by* `LeaveWorkflowTestCase`. | Maybe |
| L9 | **Who sees leave:** an application and leave card follow R4's leave balances rule: HRMO, Auditor, the employee, and leave approvers for the people they can already see (Office Heads: their office; Executives and Delegates: everyone). Office Staff and System Admin see none. The register, balances list and credit posting are HRMO (edits) and Auditor (reads). On the calendar, a leave entry opens the application. The applicant withdraws a pending application; only HRMO cancels approved leave, with a reason. | No |

## Accounts and authentication

| # | Decision | Survey? |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Employee accounts receive a **random one-time password** with `must_change_password=True`. No shared default passwords. Accounts are created in bulk by `provision_accounts` (A4). The flag is enforced by `MustChangePasswordMiddleware`: a flagged user is redirected to the password-change page from every URL except password change, logout and static files, and the flag is cleared after a successful change. *Enforced; proven by* `MustChangePasswordTestCase`. Passwords set by an admin in `/admin/` (new account or reset) also set the flag (R3). | Maybe |
| A2 | **Forgotten passwords: HRMO issues a one-time password, face to face. No email reset** (no mail server, and an emailed link would trust whoever reads the inbox). **Forgot password?** on the login page takes a username and an optional contact and always gives the same answer, so it never reveals which usernames exist; it stores at most one open request per account and 5 per IP address per hour. HRMO sees them under **Tools → Forgotten passwords** (count in the menu), or starts from the person's record for a walk-in (**Issue one-time password**). HRMO must type the ID checked and tick that the photo and name match. Issuing sets a random one-time password (A4 alphabet), shown once on a page that is not cached, with a printable slip; the old password and any session using it stop working, a login lockout on the account is lifted, the person must change it at first login (A1), and it stops working after `RESET_PASSWORD_HOURS` (default 24) if unused. Requests are audit-logged, and the reset itself as `PASSWORD_RESET` by the HRMO user, password redacted. **HRMO resets only plain employee accounts**: active, linked to a personnel record, with no role, direct permission or current or future delegation, and never their own (R10). Every other account (HRMO, Auditor, Office Head, Executive, delegates, System Admin) would let HRMO log in with more access than HRMO's own, so it stays with the emergency superuser in `/admin/` (R3); System Admin still resets accounts without HR access there. HRMO can see the password it hands out and could log in with it first; this is accepted because HRMO already sees every record, the login is audit-logged, and the person finds out at once because the password no longer works. *Proven by* `ForgotPasswordTestCase`. | Maybe |
| A3 | Session timeout: 30 minutes idle. Login rate-limiting: 5 failed attempts per username+IP locks for 15 minutes. | Maybe |
| A4 | **Bulk employee accounts and one-time password expiry:** `manage.py provision_accounts` creates an account for every person with an active appointment and no account yet (optionally one office at a time, `--office`; preview with `--dry-run`). Username = employee number (D9). Each gets a random 12-character one-time password without look-alike characters, shown once on screen and optionally on a printable slips page (`--slips`, git-ignored); only the PBKDF2 hash is stored and the audit log records each new account with the password redacted. New accounts have **no roles** (Employee: own record only); every other role is assigned separately (R3). An existing account already using the employee number but not linked to the person is skipped and reported, never taken over. Any one-time password (provisioned or reset by an admin) stops working after `ONE_TIME_PASSWORD_DAYS` (default 14) if unused: `OneTimePasswordBackend` refuses the login like a wrong password, and HRMO issues a new one. Passwords are hashed before the database transaction opens, so the audit chain lock (U6) is held only for the inserts. *Proven by* `ProvisionAccountsTestCase`. | Maybe |

## Projects

| # | Decision | Survey? |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Project Maintainer role:** Creating and editing projects needs the `manage_projects` permission, held by the `Project Maintainer` group, not by HRMO Staff. Hiring into a project (`manage_hr_records`) and defining it are separate duties, so they can sit with different offices: HRMO hires, Planning or Budget defines and funds. HRMO staff get project maintenance only by also joining the group (roles stack). The group is a protected role (R3). Project Maintainers see project details and headcounts, never personnel records or the names on a project; the roster is visible to HRMO, Auditor and Executive roles. *Proven by* `ProjectManagementTestCase`. | No |
| P2 | **Funding reference:** Every project records the certificate or obligation number from Budget / Accounting that shows it is funded (`Project.funding_reference`, required on the project form). New contracts can link only to a project that has one, which ties each project-based hire to an independent sign-off and leaves a trail if HRMO maintains projects itself. Projects recorded before this rule keep a blank reference and are closed to new hiring until one is added. *Proven by* `ProjectManagementTestCase.test_funding_reference_is_required` and `test_unfunded_or_closed_projects_are_not_offered`. | No |
| P3 | **Contracts fit inside their project:** New contracts can link only to Planned or Ongoing projects. A project-based contract must start on or after the project start and end on or before the project end (an open-ended contract is refused when the project has an end date). Extending a contract past the project end needs the Project Maintainer to extend the project first. The Project Maintainer in turn cannot move a project's dates so that active contracts fall outside them. Existing contracts keep their project when edited even after it closes. Projects use the same version check as S2. *Proven by* `ProjectManagementTestCase`. | No |
| P4 | **Open for the Planning / Budget meeting:** (1) Which office records a new project, and which confirms the money exists? That decides who joins `Project Maintainer`; if the LGU decides HRMO maintains projects itself, record that decision and date here. (2) What document proves funding, and what does its number look like? (P2 stores it as free text.) (3) Should JO / COS contracts paid from a project also link to it? Today only the Project-based type may (D7, DB constraint `engagement_project_link`). (4) Should completing a project end its active contracts automatically? Today the project page only warns. | Yes |

## Calendar

| # | Decision | Survey? |

| U5 | **Trusted proxy IP extraction & real client IP dependency:** `X-Forwarded-For` is read only when `REMOTE_ADDR` matches `TRUSTED_PROXIES`, and right to left: each proxy appends the address it received the request from, so the first hop that is not a trusted proxy is the client. Entries to its left were written by the client and are ignored; a malformed hop stops the walk. Addresses are stored in PostgreSQL's canonical form so the row hash survives a round trip. The same resolver feeds django-axes (`AXES_CLIENT_IP_CALLABLE`), so behind a proxy lockouts are per client, not per proxy. *Proven by* `Step5AuditLogTestCase.test_trusted_proxy_client_ip_resolution` and `LoginLockoutAuditTestCase`.<br>**Operational prerequisite:** The real client IP appears in audit logs and lockouts ONLY once `TRUSTED_PROXIES` in `.env` is set to the reverse proxy address. | U6 | **Signal limitations, code prohibitions, and cryptographic hash chain:**<br>- Django signals do **not** trigger on `QuerySet.update()`, `QuerySet.bulk_create()`, `QuerySet.bulk_update()`, raw SQL `cursor.execute()`, or direct `psql` queries.<br>- **Policy:** HR application code is forbidden from calling `.update()`, `.bulk_create()`, `.bulk_update()`, or raw SQL for mutating domain entities. All domain mutations must use model `.save()` or `.delete()`. Developers must use `docker compose down -v` to reset a local development database.<br>- **Cryptographic SHA-256 hash chain:** A linear hash chain (`prev_hash`, `row_hash`) links each record in `AuditLog`. Each `row_hash` is computed across all row fields (including `request_id` and `session_key_hash`) plus `prev_hash`. Appends are serialized by a transaction-scoped PostgreSQL advisory lock (`pg_advisory_xact_lock`), and the chain head is read after the lock is taken, so concurrent writers always link to each other. (The earlier `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` on the last row let two concurrent writers link to the same parent, which broke verification for good; *proven fixed by* `AuditChainConcurrencyTestCase`.) Verification is always a full scan from the first record. It runs only from the `verify_audit_chain` management command or from the auditor-only "Re-verify all" button on `/audit/` (POST, at most once per minute across all users, each run logged as a `verify_chain` audit event). Both store the result (time, valid/broken, record count, anchor status) in the one-row `AuditChainStatus` table. `/audit/` never verifies on page load: it shows that stored result with its timestamp, plus how many newer entries it does not cover, or "Not verified yet" if none exists. The UI says "tamper-evident" only when the stored result passed with a verified anchor. The stored result is written by the application, so whoever controls the app role could fake it; the authoritative check is `verify_audit_chain --anchor` run with the externally held anchor (U8), and `/audit/` says so. *Enforced; proven by* `AuditVerificationStatusTestCase` and `AuditHardeningTestCase`. | No |
| U7 | **Audit retention policy & no-purge design:** The web application cannot purge or truncate audit rows by design (enforced by the U2 PostgreSQL triggers and DB privilege revocation; see U2 for which parts have an automated test). Audit-log retention policy (e.g., retention window, active DB vs. cold storage archiving) is an open question to align with the municipal Data Privacy Officer (DPO). | Yes |
| U8 | **Nightly encrypted export of audit table & external anchor:** Nightly encrypted export of the `hr_auditlog` table is automatically exported and streamed to LGU-held storage (e.g., external encrypted backup volume or isolated municipal share) to protect audit integrity even in the event of host failure or database corruption.<br>- **Export manifest anchor line:** Each nightly export includes a manifest containing the latest anchor line: `ANCHOR: <ID>:<ROW_HASH>` with timestamp generated by `python manage.py audit_anchor`.<br>- **External custody by DPO / Auditor:** The Municipal Data Privacy Officer (DPO) or external auditor must record and store the latest anchor value somewhere completely outside the server (e.g., off-site ledger, paper log, or isolated municipal email record). Storing the anchor externally ensures that even an attacker with full root and database access cannot alter historical records without detection, as verification with `python manage.py verify_audit_chain --anchor ID:HASH` will immediately detect any tampering. | No |

## Go-Live Checklist (Pre-Production Deployment)

The system currently runs in a prototype profile with synthetic test data. Prior to processing genuine municipal personnel data, all items on this checklist must be verified and implemented:

1. **Fake-ID guard removal (Decision D1). Requires DPO approval.**
   - What exists today: each government ID field has a database `CheckConstraint` (`person_<field>_fake_only`) that only accepts values starting with `00`, plus a form check controlled by `PROTOTYPE_FAKE_DATA_ONLY`.
   - Setting `PROTOTYPE_FAKE_DATA_ONLY=0` **only skips the form check**. The database constraint still rejects every real ID, in the web form, in the admin, and in any import (`FakeIdGuardTestCase`). The flag does not change the "Prototype · fake data only" banner, which is hard-coded in the templates.
   - **No real-ID format validation exists yet.** Before go-live, all of the following are required, in this order:
     1. Written DPO approval to load real government ID numbers.
     2. Real-ID format validation (e.g. TIN 9 or 12 digits, GSIS, PhilHealth, SSS, Pag-IBIG formats), written and tested.
     3. A deliberate migration that removes the five `person_*_fake_only` constraints, reviewed and applied on purpose. Do not try to bypass the constraints with an environment flag.
     4. Remove the prototype banners from the templates.

2. **Django DEBUG Mode:**
   - Set `DJANGO_DEBUG=0` in `.env`.
   - Disables verbose debug tracebacks and the Django technical error reporter, preventing credential or schema leaks.
   - Automatically deactivates the `create_demo_users` management command.
   - The Docker image already runs gunicorn, collects static files and serves `/static/` with WhiteNoise (H6). Do not use the compose file's `runserver --insecure` on the real server.

3. **HTTPS & Transport Security:**
   - Set `DJANGO_HTTPS=1` in `.env`.
   - Activates `SECURE_SSL_REDIRECT=True`, `SESSION_COOKIE_SECURE=True`, `CSRF_COOKIE_SECURE=True`, and HSTS headers.
   - Configure reverse proxy (Caddy / Nginx) with automated internal TLS certificates or municipal authority certificates for all intranet and mobile traffic.

4. **TRUSTED_PROXIES Configuration (Decision U5):**
   - Update `TRUSTED_PROXIES` in `.env` to the exact IP address of the reverse proxy container or gateway (e.g., `172.18.0.x` or loopback).
   - Ensures client IP resolution from `X-Forwarded-For` and `X-Request-ID` tracing are accepted strictly from the reverse proxy, while rejecting spoofed headers from direct client connections.
   - Also required for login lockouts to be per client rather than per proxy (django-axes uses the same resolver).

5. **Secrets & Production Credentials:**
   - Generate a cryptographically random `DJANGO_SECRET_KEY` (minimum 50 characters).
   - Change `POSTGRES_PASSWORD` and `APP_DB_PASSWORD` to distinct high-entropy secrets stored exclusively in restricted `.env` files outside version control.
   - Purge all prototype/demo seed users and initialize genuine administrative accounts with forced password changes on first login.

6. **Automated Encrypted Backups & External Anchor Custody (Decisions U8, B1-B5):**
   - Implement nightly automated `pg_dump` encrypted with the municipal public key (`gpg` / `age`).
   - Implement nightly incremental sync of `/data/media` (personnel document scans volume).
   - Implement nightly export of `hr_auditlog` including the anchor line output (`python manage.py audit_anchor --raw`).
   - Establish an operational protocol for the Municipal DPO or external auditor to record the latest anchor string outside the server (e.g., printed ledger or off-site storage).

7. **Data Privacy Officer (DPO) Approval (Decisions U7, S4, S9):**
   - Formal sign-off on compliance with the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173) and municipal employee consent forms.
   - Formal policy on audit log retention and archival schedule (active PostgreSQL window vs. offline cold storage).
   - Decision on `django-axes` attempted username storage vs. ephemeral cache rate-limiting.
   - Confirmation that wet-signed paper records remain the legal master copies filed in physical municipal archives.

8. **Municipal Hosting & Physical Infrastructure:**
   - Deploy on dedicated municipal server hardware equipped with Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS) to mitigate local power outages.
   - House server in a secure, climate-controlled, locked municipal IT room with restricted physical access.
   - Restrict database and web ports to the municipal staff VLAN / intranet.

9. **Network exposure decision:**
   - Decide whether the site is reachable only on the office network (or VPN), or from the public internet so staff can use mobile data. Office network only is the safer first step; public access requires every item below as well.

10. **Second login step (MFA) for privileged roles (not built):**
    - HRMO Staff, Auditor, System Admin and the emergency superuser can see or change every record. Add a second factor (e.g. an authenticator app) for these roles before go-live, and before any public exposure in any case. Sole exception: the fake-data Railway review until 2026-11-13 (H3).

11. **Privacy Impact Assessment and NPC registration:**
    - The DPO completes a Privacy Impact Assessment for this system and confirms whether the municipality's registration with the National Privacy Commission must cover it.

12. **Phased account rollout (Decision A4):**
    - First HRMO, Auditor and Office Heads. Employee accounts follow office by office (`provision_accounts --office`) once self-service (Step 10) and HTTPS exist. The HRMO password-reset screen is built (A2).

13. **Named maintainer and penetration test:**
    - The LGU names who applies security updates (Django 5.2 LTS is supported until April 2028), checks backups, resets passwords and watches the audit log. A prototype developer working part-time is not enough for live personal data.
    - A penetration test (e.g. OWASP ZAP or Burp Suite) logged in under each role, before real data is loaded.
